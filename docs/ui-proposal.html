From canvas to running lab in under five clicks.

A high-fidelity walkthrough of the LabForge experience — from the dashboard landing, through the topology canvas, into CVE pinning and validation, all the way to a live multi-VM run. The goal: make spinning up a realistic security lab feel less like writing YAML and more like sketching on a napkin.

Why pictorial nodes over rectangular cards. Cybersecurity diagrams have a visual language already — racks, firewalls, PLCs, attackers in hoodies. Honouring that vocabulary reduces cognitive load and helps cross-functional teams (red team / blue team / IT / OT) read the same diagram. Zones use translucent shapes instead of rigid containers so groupings feel like intent, not bureaucracy.

Why a sheet, not toasts. Validation results are a deliverable, not a notification. Listing them densely with their rule name and fix CTA turns review into a checklist. Info-level rows reassure users that the system understood their intent (e.g. "3 agents will auto-register"). The "generate anyway" checkbox prevents click-fatigue on yellow warnings while keeping red errors hard blockers.

Why the live panel matters most. Most labs feel "done" the moment vagrant up starts — but provisioning is where things actually break. Showing per-VM progress with role chips and a tailing log turns a 6-minute wait into something diagnosable. The destroy button is intentionally red and one click away — labs should be cheap to throw away and rebuild.
